# Canary Islands in Winter: three islands to escape the cold

There is a moment of the year when you start looking at the map with different eyes. When autumn tightens and winter approaches, the question stops being "where do we go" and becomes "where is it not cold".

And for that question, the Canary Islands have an answer that has worked for years: <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">volcanoes, dunes and beaches with temperatures that rarely drop below 20 degrees</strong>, just over two hours by flight from the Spanish mainland.

It is not just about sun, either. It is the complete combination: landscapes that look like another planet, food with its own identity, charming villages and the chance to combine beach and mountain on the same day. In December you can have breakfast looking at Mount Teide and swim at noon in a cove in the south.

In this article we suggest <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">3 islands with their routes</strong>: Tenerife for those who want a bit of everything, Gran Canaria for those looking for contrasts and Lanzarote for those who prefer something different.

> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - The Canary Islands keep temperatures around 20 degrees in winter, a short flight away from mainland Spain and Portugal.
> - Tenerife is the most complete island: Mount Teide (Spain's highest peak at 3,715 metres), southern beaches and villages like Masca.
> - Gran Canaria combines the Maspalomas dunes, nature inland and a capital with an urban vibe.
> - Lanzarote offers a unique volcanic landscape, with Timanfaya park and the work of César Manrique.
> - A week allows you to combine two islands; with fewer days, the best move is to focus on one.

## Why the Canary Islands in winter?

The Canary Islands do not have a harsh winter because their climate is mild all year round.

In coastal areas, winter temperatures average around 20 degrees, with highs above that figure most days. The sea water is fresh but manageable, and natural pools and sheltered coves are perfectly usable.

But the strongest argument is variety.

In just a few days you can climb Teide, walk through dunes, visit wineries, tour mountain villages and finish the day with a dinner of wrinkled potatoes with mojo sauce while watching the sunset. Few European destinations offer that mix, especially in the middle of December.

Flights from Madrid, Barcelona, Porto or Lisbon are also short and frequent, which makes the Canary Islands a plan for a long weekend or a full week almost without thinking.

## Tenerife: Teide and the south

Tenerife is the island of contrasts, in capital letters.

The absolute star is <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Mount Teide, at 3,715 metres the highest peak in Spain</strong> and the heart of a national park declared a UNESCO World Heritage site. Reaching the base of the volcano is already impressive; taking the cable car to the upper station is one of those experiences you remember. On a clear day, the view spans other islands and a volcanic landscape that does not look of this world.

The south of the island concentrates beaches and resorts: Los Cristianos, Las Américas and Costa Adeje, with golden sand, beach bars and a tourist atmosphere. It is the most convenient base if you want to combine beach and excursions.

But Tenerife has much more. The village of <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Masca</strong>, perched on a ravine, is one of the most famous postcards of the archipelago. The historic centre of La Laguna is a World Heritage site and deserves half a day. And the Los Gigantes coast offers sheer cliffs that are best enjoyed by boat or from the viewpoints.

## Gran Canaria: dunes and nature

Gran Canaria sells itself with one phrase: on a single island you can find beach, desert, forest and mountain.

The classic plan starts in the south, at <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Maspalomas</strong>, where golden sand dunes reach the sea. It is one of the most photographed landscapes in the whole archipelago and it is explored on foot or by camel, depending on your sense of adventure. The sunset walk from Maspalomas lighthouse is hard to beat.

The island's interior is a different story: ravines, pine forests and mountain villages like Tejeda, crowned by <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Roque Nublo</strong>, a monolith over 80 metres tall that is the island's symbol. The road climbing from the south is a scenic route in itself, with viewpoints over the summits.

And the capital, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, adds the urban component: the Vegueta neighbourhood, Las Canteras beach and the energy of a city that is alive all year round.

**Best for:** travellers looking for contrasts who do not want to give up nature, beach and city in a single trip.

## Lanzarote: volcanoes and art

Lanzarote is the most different island, and that is precisely what makes it special.

Its volcanic landscape, the result of historic eruptions, has been turned into a work of art thanks to <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">César Manrique</strong>, the local artist who integrated architecture into nature. <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Timanfaya</strong> park, with its craters and lava fields, is the must-see: bring a pan for the tasting of food cooked with the geothermal heat of the ground.

Manrique's legacy can be visited at several points: the Jameos del Agua, a volcanic tube turned into an art space; the Cueva de los Verdes, with its walk through lava; and the Mirador del Río, with views over the Chinijo archipelago. All are accessible and all surprise.

Lanzarote's beaches are wilder: Famara, with its cliffs, and Papagayo, with coves of clear sand, are the favourites. The villages of La Geria, in the wine region planted in volcanic ash, complete an island that invites you to slow down.

**Best for:** travellers who already know the Canary Islands or are looking for something genuinely different, with volcanic landscapes and art instead of classic beach tourism.

## Which island is best for you?

The short answer: it depends on what you are looking for.

Choose <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Tenerife</strong> if you want total variety and it is your first time in the Canaries. It is the most complete island, with Teide as the star plan and the south as a beach base.

Choose <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Gran Canaria</strong> if you fancy dunes, nature and a city in a single trip, with easy car routes between the south and the summits.

Choose <strong style="color: #ff7f50ff;">Lanzarote</strong> if you prefer a unique landscape, a relaxed pace and a more cultural experience built around the volcano and Manrique.

If you have a week, the classic combination is Tenerife plus another island (Lanzarote or Gran Canaria), with an inter-island flight of under an hour. With 3 or 4 days, better to focus on one island and avoid wasting time on transfers.

## Frequently asked questions about the Canary Islands in winter

### Is the weather good in the Canary Islands in December and January?

Yes, on most days. Coastal temperatures average around 20 degrees, with sunny days going higher. The water is fresh, so it is best to choose coves and natural pools. At the summits, such as Teide, it is cold and it can snow, which is worth planning for when packing.

### How many days do I need for a Canary Islands trip?

With 4 days you can enjoy one island at a relaxed pace. With a week you can combine two islands, and with 10 days even three. It is not worth changing islands every two days: transfer time and luggage eat hours of enjoyment.

### Which is the best Canary island in winter?

They all work in winter thanks to the mild climate. For a first visit, Tenerife is the most complete option. Lanzarote and Fuerteventura offer more tranquillity and different landscapes, and Gran Canaria the best balance between beach, nature and city.
