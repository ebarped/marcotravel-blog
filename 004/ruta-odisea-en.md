# The Odyssey Route: 15 Days from Troy to Ithaca Following Odysseus

There is a story that has been told for nearly 2,800 years: a man who just wants to go home, and an entire Mediterranean standing in his way. Ten years of war in Troy, ten more years of storms, monsters, sorceresses, and angry gods. That story is <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">The Odyssey</span>, that man is <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">Odysseus</span>, and that Mediterranean... is practically the same one you can cross today by plane, ferry, and rental car.

Because here is the best part: Homer never gave coordinates, but historians and geographers have spent centuries cross-referencing the poem with the real map, and the consensus is surprisingly solid. The rocks a furious cyclops hurled into the sea are still there. The cave where a goddess hid a treasure is too. And the island that Odysseus took twenty years to step foot on again has a name, a port, and direct flights: these are the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">real places of the Odyssey</span>, and you can visit them.

With all that material, we have designed a real 15-day itinerary through Turkey, Italy, and Greece that follows the *nostos* —the return home— episode by episode: a literary route through the Mediterranean as mythological as it is visitable. It is not a literal cruise through the poem: it is a real journey, with suitcases, ferries, and schedules, that also happens to tell one of the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">best stories ever written</span>.

## The Odyssey Itinerary at a Glance

* **Days 1-2 · Turkey** — Çanakkale and the archaeological site of Troy.
* **Days 3-9 · Southern Italy** — Aci Trezza (cyclopes), Strait of Messina (Scylla and Charybdis), Aeolian Islands (Aeolus), Naples coast (sirens and underworld), and Mount Circeo (Circe).
* **Days 10-15 · Greece** — Corfu (the Phaeacians) and Ithaca (the palace of Odysseus).

---

## Chapter 1: Where It All Begins (Turkey)

Every great story needs a starting point, and Odysseus's is a battlefield you can still walk on today.

### Day 1 · Çanakkale and the Dardanelles Strait
Here, three millennia ago, an exhausted army and a hero with more wit than patience devised the trick that decided a war: the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">wooden horse</span>. Arrival in this lively coastal city, with the Dardanelles Strait, which separates Europe from Asia, as the first postcard of the trip.

### Day 2 · Troy
The siege ends and the true tale begins: the return. A full day to explore the superimposed walls of the **Hisarlik** archaeological site, a <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">UNESCO World Heritage Site</span>, and its museum, which turns myth into something you can touch.

---

## Chapter 2: Monsters, Gods, and Magic in Southern Italy

If The Odyssey has a section of pure adventure, this is it. Sicily, Calabria, Campania, and Lazio concentrate the most famous episodes of the poem —and the most visited real-world settings of the entire route— one after another.

### Day 3 · Flight to Catania (Sicily)
Transition toward the western Mediterranean. Check-in in Catania and a first Sicilian dinner: the rest before the mythological storm that begins tomorrow.

### Day 4 · Aci Trezza, the Island of the Cyclopes
The episode everyone remembers: Odysseus intoxicates and blinds Polyphemus, escaping his cave clinging to the belly of his own sheep before the giant, maddened with rage, hurls huge boulders at his ship. Those boulders are still there: they are the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">Faraglioni dei Ciclopi</span>, eight masses of basalt off this fishing village, with Mount Etna keeping watch in the background.

### Day 5 · Strait of Messina: Scylla and Charybdis
The most feared crossing of the entire trip, between two monsters impossible to dodge at once: **Scylla**, the six-headed cliff that devoured sailors, and **Charybdis**, the whirlpool capable of swallowing an entire ship. Odysseus had to choose the lesser of two evils and sacrifice six of his men. Today they are two viewpoints facing each other —Torre Faro in Sicily and Scilla in Calabria— before boarding the ferry north.

### Day 6 · Aeolian Islands, the Realm of Aeolus
The god of the winds hands Odysseus victory on a silver platter: a leather bag containing all unfavorable winds, leaving only the free zephyr to push him home. With Ithaca already in sight, the crew's curiosity ruins everything. A day of sailing through the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">volcanic archipelago of Lipari</span>, with an afternoon excursion to see Stromboli in active eruption, almost live.

### Day 7 · Sorrento Coast
Ferry and road travel toward the Sorrentino Peninsula, featuring waters as beautiful as they were treacherous for ancient navigators. An afternoon of rest facing the Tyrrhenian Sea, before the most myth-heavy day of the entire route.

### Day 8 · Naples Coast: Sirens and Underworld
Double episode, a single day. Off the islets of Li Galli, Odysseus has himself tied to the mast of his own ship to listen to the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">song of the sirens</span> without plunging into the sea. Nearby, Lake Avernus, next to Cumae, was viewed by Greeks and Romans as the very gateway to Hades: the place where the hero descends to speak with the ghost of the seer Tiresias about what awaits him. Coastal viewpoints in the morning, Phlegrean Fields and the smoking crater of Avernus in the afternoon.

### Day 9 · Mount Circeo, the Island of Circe
The most famous sorceress in Greek mythology turns part of the crew into pigs and detains Odysseus for a whole year —the hero himself, always in a rush to return, takes twelve months to leave—. Ascent to the promontory of San Felice Circeo, its walled medieval village, and the cyclopean remains of its ancient acropolis; evening transfer to Rome to close the Italian chapter in grand style.

## Chapter 3: Hospitality and the Return to Ithaca (Greece)

After the chaos, calm. The final part of the poem —and of this route— is about hospitality, the final obstacles, and, at last, coming home.

### Day 10 · Flight to Corfu, the Land of the Phaeacians
The plot twist of the entire poem: shipwrecked, naked, and exhausted after the storm sent by Poseidon, Odysseus washes up on the beach of Scheria and is found by Princess Nausicaa. Flight to Corfu and an afternoon exploring its <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">Venetian-style old town</span>, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Day 11 · Corfu: Nausicaa's Bay
King Alcinous listens to the complete account of Odysseus's adventures and, deeply moved, promises him the fleet that will finally take him home. Excursion to the monasteries and coves of Paleokastritsa, the place local tradition links to that first encounter between the hero and the princess.

### Day 12 · Descent through the Ionian Sea
While Odysseus sleeps soundly, a Phaeacian ship transports him and deposits him, in secret, on the shores of his own homeland: the ending he had spent twenty years waiting for. A combined ferry and road trip winding along the Greek continental coast and neighboring islands, heading toward the central Ionian.

### Day 13 · Arrival in Ithaca
Odysseus awakens on his own island and, at first, doesn't even recognize it: Athena has shrouded it in mist to protect him. The first ally he encounters is his faithful swineherd Eumaeus. Ferry entry into the port of Vathý, disembarkation, and settling in facing the natural gulf.

### Day 14 · Ithaca, the End of the Journey
The settings that give meaning to the previous fourteen days: the hill of Pilikata, identified with the <span style="color: #FF7F50; text-decoration: underline;">palace of Odysseus</span>, overlooking three bays and Cephalonia cut out against the background; and the mysterious Cave of the Nymphs, where the hero, with Athena's help, hides the treasures given to him by the Phaeacians. Archaeological tour of the island and an afternoon of calm facing the same Ionian Sea that kept him away from home for two decades.

### Day 15 · From Ithaca to Athens
The circle closes: twenty years of war, exile, and trials are left behind, and the hero finally reclaims his home. Morning ferry from Ithaca to the mainland port and road transfer to Athens airport for the return flight.
