# From Troy to Ithaca: The Odyssey you can actually travel

There's a story we've been telling for almost 2,800 years: a man who just wants to get home, and an entire Mediterranean determined to stop him. Ten years of war in Troy, ten more years of storms, monsters, sorceresses, and moody gods. That story is <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">The Odyssey</span>, that man is <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Odysseus</span>, and that Mediterranean... is pretty much the same one you can cross today, minus the storms and monsters.

And here's the best part: Homer never left a map, but historians and geographers have spent centuries cross-referencing the poem with real geography, and the results line up so well it's hard not to want to trace them yourself. The rocks a furious Cyclops hurled into the sea are still there. So is the cave where a goddess hid a treasure. And the island it took Odysseus twenty years to reach has a name, and you can visit it — what are you waiting for?

With all that material, at <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Marco</span> we've put together a real 15-day itinerary through Turkey, Italy, and Greece that follows the *nostos* (the journey home) episode by episode: a literary route across the Mediterranean as mythical as it is visitable, and one that happens to tell one of the greatest stories ever written.

## The Odyssey ttinerary at a glance

* **Days 1-2 · Turkey**: Çanakkale and the archaeological site of Troy.
* **Days 3-9 · Southern Italy**: Aci Trezza (the Cyclops), the Strait of Messina (Scylla and Charybdis), the Aeolian Islands (Aeolus), the coast of Naples (Sirens and the Underworld), and Monte Circeo (Circe).
* **Days 10-15 · Greece**: Corfu (the Phaeacians) and Ithaca (the palace of Odysseus).


## Chapter 1: Where it all begins

Every great story needs a starting point, and Odysseus's is a battlefield you can still walk today.

### Day 1 · Çanakkale and the Dardanelles Strait
Here, three thousand years ago, an exhausted army and a hero with more cunning than patience came up with the trick that decided a war: the <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">wooden horse</span>. Arrival in this lively coastal city, with the Dardanelles Strait — separating Europe from Asia — as the trip's first postcard moment.

### Day 2 · Troy
The siege ends and the real story begins: the return home. A full day exploring the layered walls of the <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Hisarlik</span> site, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its museum, which turns myth into something you can almost touch.

![Archaeological site of Troy, Hisarlik, Turkey](https://images.pexels.com/photos/12646217/pexels-photo-12646217.jpeg)

## Chapter 2: Monsters, Gods, and magic in southern Italy

If the Odyssey has a pure-adventure stretch, this is it. Sicily, Calabria, Campania, and Lazio pack in the poem's most famous episodes (and the route's most-visited real-life settings), one after another.

### Day 3 · Flight to Catania
The crossing into the western Mediterranean. Settling into Catania for a first Sicilian dinner: a calm evening before tomorrow's mythological storm.

### Day 4 · Aci Trezza, the island of the cyclops
The episode everyone remembers: Odysseus gets Polyphemus drunk and blinds him, then escapes his cave clinging to the belly of his own sheep before the enraged giant hurls enormous rocks at his ship. Those rocks are still there: the <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Faraglioni dei Ciclopi</span>, eight basalt sea stacks off this fishing village, with Mount Etna keeping watch in the background.

![Faraglioni dei Ciclopi off Aci Trezza, Sicily](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18782344/pexels-photo-18782344.jpeg)

### Day 5 · Strait of Messina: Scylla and Charybdis
The most feared passage of the whole journey, between two monsters impossible to dodge at once: **Scylla**, the six-headed cliff-monster that devoured sailors, and **Charybdis**, the whirlpool capable of swallowing an entire ship. Odysseus had to pick the lesser evil and sacrifice six of his men. Today it's two lookout points facing each other: Torre Faro in Sicily and Scilla in Calabria.

### Day 6 · The Aeolian Islands, Kingdom of Aeolus
The god of the winds hands Odysseus victory on a silver platter: a bag holding every unfavorable wind, leaving only the west wind free to push him home. With Ithaca already in sight, the crew's curiosity ruins everything. A day sailing through the <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">volcanic Aeolian archipelago</span>, with a sunset excursion to watch <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Stromboli</span> erupting, almost live.

### Day 7 · The Sorrento Coast
Ferry and road transfer to the Sorrentine Peninsula, waters as beautiful as they were treacherous for ancient sailors. An afternoon of rest by the Tyrrhenian Sea, before the most myth-packed day of the whole route.

### Day 8 · The Naples coast: sirens and the underworld
Two episodes, one single day. Off the Li Galli islets, Odysseus has himself tied to his own ship's mast so he can hear the <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Sirens' song</span> without throwing himself into the sea. Nearby, Lake Averno, next to Cumae, was for Greeks and Romans the very gateway to Hades: the place where the hero descends to speak with the ghost of the prophet Tiresias about what awaits him. Coastal lookouts in the morning, the Phlegraean Fields and Averno's smoking crater in the afternoon.

### Day 9 · Monte Circeo, Circe's Island
Mythology's most famous sorceress turns part of the crew into pigs and keeps Odysseus there for a full year. A climb up the San Felice Circeo promontory, its walled medieval village, and the cyclopean remains of its ancient acropolis; overnight transfer to Rome to close out the Italian chapter in style.


## Chapter 3: Hospitality and the return to Ithaca

After the chaos, calm. The final part of the poem — and of this route — is about hospitality, the last obstacles, and finally, coming home.

### Day 10 · Flight to Corfu, tand of the Phaeacians
The plot twist of the whole poem: shipwrecked, naked, and exhausted after the storm Poseidon sends his way, Odysseus washes up on the beach of Scheria and is found by Princess Nausicaa. Flight to Corfu and an afternoon exploring its <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Venetian-style old town</span>, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Day 11 · Corfu: Nausicaa's Bay
King Alcinous listens to the full account of Odysseus's adventures and, moved, promises him the fleet that will finally carry him home. An excursion to the monasteries and coves of Paleokastritsa, the spot local tradition links to that first encounter between the hero and the princess.

### Day 12 · Descent through the Ionian Sea
While Odysseus sleeps deeply, a Phaeacian ship carries him and sets him down, secretly, on the shores of his own homeland: the ending he'd been waiting twenty years for. A combined ferry-and-road route winding along the Greek mainland coast and nearby islands, heading toward the central Ionian.

### Day 13 · Arrival in Ithaca
Odysseus wakes on his own island and, at first, doesn't even recognize it: Athena has shrouded it in mist to protect him. The first ally he finds is his loyal swineherd, Eumaeus. Ferry arrival at the port of Vathý, disembarkation, and check-in overlooking the natural bay.

### Day 14 · Ithaca, the journey's end
The settings that give meaning to the previous fourteen days: Pilikata hill, identified with <span style="color: #FF7F50; font-weight: bold;">Odysseus's palace</span>, with views over three bays and Cephalonia in the distance; and the mysterious Cave of the Nymphs, where the hero, with Athena's help, hides the treasures the Phaeacians gave him. An archaeological tour of the island and a quiet afternoon by the same Ionian Sea that kept him from home for two decades.

![Vathy Bay](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Ithaki-Vathy.jpg/960px-Ithaki-Vathy.jpg?utm_source=commons.wikimedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail)

### Day 15 · From Ithaca to Athens
The circle closes: twenty years of war, exile, and trials are behind him, and the hero finally reclaims his home. Morning ferry from Ithaca to the mainland port and road transfer to Athens airport for the flight home.
